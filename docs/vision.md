# Northstar — What We Are Building

Northstar is a **personal discovery, learning and growth companion**.

Its job is not to manage my life, track my productivity, or tell me whether I am improving.

Its job is much simpler:

> **Find, filter, connect and recommend things that are genuinely worth my attention, so that I can learn, explore, think, practise, create and experience more of the world.**

I already maintain my own private notes, plans and progress tracking elsewhere. Northstar should **not try to become that system**.

Northstar is the layer that sits before that:

**World → Information → Northstar's filtering and recommendations → My attention → My own decisions and notes**

The core value is therefore **curation and discovery**, not tracking.

---

# 1. The problem Northstar solves

There is far more interesting information, knowledge, research, culture, history, art, science, people and experiences available than one person can consume.

The problem is therefore not:

> "How can I consume more?"

It is:

> **"How can I discover the small fraction that is actually worth my attention?"**

And beyond information:

> **"What interesting things could I learn, think about, experience, practise or make that I might otherwise never encounter?"**

Northstar should continuously expose me to worthwhile possibilities without overwhelming me.

It should help me:

- stay aware of important developments
- discover research worth reading
- become a better researcher
- learn outside my field
- understand history and philosophy
- understand people and society
- discover books, movies, music and art
- find interesting people and their stories
- discover hobbies and things to make
- encounter unusual ideas
- make connections between unrelated fields
- occasionally go down a deep rabbit hole
- find things worth experiencing in real life

---

# 2. Northstar is NOT a productivity tracker

This is fundamental.

Northstar should **not track my life**.

Do not build a system around:

- streaks
- XP
- points
- scores
- completion percentages
- productivity metrics
- daily task completion
- habit tracking
- quotas
- "you missed this"
- overdue tasks
- accumulating backlogs
- guilt
- forced journaling
- mandatory reflection
- progress dashboards

I maintain my own private notes and tracking.

Northstar does not need to know whether I actually read the paper, watched the movie, completed the problem, visited the place, or learned the skill.

Its job is to say:

> "Here are some things that might be worth doing."

Then I decide what I actually do.

---

# 3. Northstar should feel like a personal intellectual compass

The name Northstar is intentional.

A North Star gives direction, but does not force a route.

Northstar should therefore provide:

- signals
- recommendations
- possibilities
- interesting questions
- connections
- things to explore
- things worth trying

It should not provide:

- rigid schedules
- obligations
- performance metrics

The system should make life **richer**, not busier.

# 5. Research Radar

The research radar is particularly important.

Northstar should use multiple signals rather than relying only on one paper source.

Important sources include:

### arXiv

Use arXiv for the broad research stream.

But do not simply show every paper matching keywords.

The system should semantically filter papers based on:

- current research interests
- long-term research interests
- important adjacent areas
- research fundamentals
- potential usefulness
- novelty
- relevance
- significance

The output should be a **small curated radar**, not an arXiv dump.

---

### AlphaXiv / Alpha Arxiv

Use AlphaXiv as another research signal.

In particular, **trending papers should be considered**, because a paper may be worth knowing about even if it was not predicted to be highly relevant from the user's static interests.

Trending signals should therefore complement semantic interest filtering.

The system should be able to distinguish:

> "This paper matches your interests."

from:

> "This paper is becoming important or widely discussed."

Both are valuable for a researcher.

---

### Hugging Face

Use Hugging Face as another research and practical AI signal.

This can include:

- papers
- trending research
- models
- datasets
- important new releases
- notable technical developments
- practical implementations

The purpose is not merely to find popular models.

It is to identify developments that could help someone become a better AI researcher.

---

# 6. The research radar should optimize for becoming a better researcher

The question should not simply be:

> "Is this paper about something I like?"

A stronger question is:

> **"Would knowing this make me a better researcher?"**

That can mean different things.

A paper might be valuable because:

- it is directly relevant to current research
- it introduces an important method
- it challenges a common assumption
- it contains a useful theoretical idea
- it introduces a strong benchmark
- it exposes a failure mode
- it provides a useful experimental methodology
- it connects two fields
- it is becoming influential
- it contains a research direction
- it is an important negative result
- it teaches a fundamental concept
- it provides a useful tool or dataset
- it represents an important shift in the field

Therefore the research radar should deliberately include both:

**direct relevance** and **research development value**.

# 13. Weekly recommendations are intentionally open-ended

Weekly content should **not** mean:

> one research task + one knowledge task + one culture task + one personal-development task.

That would turn Northstar into a checklist.

Instead, each week Northstar should generate a **small collection of genuinely interesting things**.

A week might contain:

- an AI research fundamentals problem
- one research paper worth reading
- a philosophical question
- a historical story
- a movie connected to philosophy
- the life of an artist
- three songs from that artist
- an interesting scientific question
- a new hobby to try
- a conversation idea
- an unusual piece of technology
- a book chapter
- a random connection between two fields

Another week might contain completely different things.

**Anything is acceptable if it is genuinely worthwhile.**

The weekly system should therefore optimize for:

> **"What would make this week intellectually, creatively or experientially richer?"**

rather than category coverage.

# 16. On-demand recommendations

Northstar should also respond to things I mention during the week.

For example:

> "I suddenly want to understand Japanese history."

or:

> "I want to try photography."

or:

> "I am free this weekend. Give me something interesting."

or:

> "I liked that philosophy recommendation. Give me something related."

or:

> "I want to explore this again next week."

The system should be able to carry that intent into the appropriate future recommendation.

This is **human-directed adaptation**, not automated task tracking.

---

# 17. No need for internal tracking tags

Do not design Northstar around me maintaining statuses or tags.

I am not going to manually maintain:

- completed
- skipped
- in progress
- backlog
- scores
- progress
- habit state
- personal reflection state

My own private notes handle that.

Northstar's internal state, if technically necessary for its recommendation pipeline, should remain an implementation detail and should not become a user-facing life-management system.

The important information for recommendation generation is simply:

- what I explicitly told Northstar I care about
- what I explicitly want to explore
- what I previously said I liked or disliked
- what I asked to revisit
- what kinds of recommendations seem useful

Do not create unnecessary personal tracking.

---

# 18. Feedback

Feedback should be lightweight and natural.

Examples:

> "More like this."

> "Less of this."

> "I already know this."

> "This was interesting."

> "Too basic."

> "Too technical."

> "I want to explore this later."

> "I want this again next week."

> "Don't recommend this kind of thing."

Northstar should use this to improve future recommendations.

The system should not turn feedback into a complicated scoring model.

---

# 19. Familiarity, adjacency and surprise

Northstar should balance:

### Familiar

Things I already care about.

### Adjacent

Things connected to what I care about.

### Unexpected

Things I would not normally search for but may find valuable.

The third category is essential.

A recommendation system that only knows what I already like will eventually create a filter bubble.

Northstar should occasionally make me think:

> "I would never have searched for this myself."

That is one of the main reasons the system exists.

# 21. Information quality

Northstar should be selective.

Do not optimize for:

- number of items
- engagement
- click-through
- content volume
- novelty alone

Optimize for:

- relevance
- quality
- significance
- credibility
- usefulness
- intellectual value
- cultural value
- novelty
- diversity
- potential for deeper exploration

A strong day with five excellent discoveries is better than a day with fifty mediocre links.

---

# 22. The fundamental loop

Northstar should ultimately create this experience:

**Discover something → become curious → explore it → maybe practise/create/experience it → decide what matters → move on or go deeper.**

The system does not need to know what happened afterward.

Its responsibility is to make the **first discovery and next choice better**.

---

# 23. The deeper philosophy

Northstar is ultimately about **attention**.

A person's life is partly shaped by what they repeatedly notice.

If all of their attention goes toward:

- work
- algorithms
- social media
- immediate obligations
- whatever happens to be trending

their world can become narrow.

Northstar should deliberately widen the field of attention.

It should help someone notice:

- a paper
- an idea
- a scientist
- a philosopher
- a historical event
- a country
- an artist
- a song
- a movie
- a book
- a scientific mystery
- a hobby
- a conversation
- a place
- a problem worth solving
- something worth making

The system is therefore not trying to make someone **do more**.

It is trying to help them **notice better things**.

---

# 24. The desired outcome

If Northstar works well, after months of using it a person should not necessarily have a higher productivity score.

Instead, they should gradually have:

- better research taste
- broader knowledge
- stronger mental models
- better understanding of the world
- better conversations
- greater emotional maturity
- richer cultural taste
- more curiosity
- more interesting experiences
- more creative ideas
- more things they have tried
- more connections between fields
- a clearer sense of what they genuinely care about

And importantly:

> **They should still feel that their life belongs to them.**

Northstar should provide possibilities, not obligations.

---

# 25. Guiding principle for every future decision

Whenever you modify or extend Northstar, use this question:

> **Does this help the person discover, understand, practise, create or experience something genuinely worthwhile, while respecting their attention and freedom?**

If yes, it may belong in Northstar.

If it mainly makes the person:

- track more
- measure more
- consume more
- complete more
- maintain more
- feel guilty
- follow an artificial schedule

then it probably does not belong.

**Northstar is not a system for managing a person.**

**It is a system for helping a person discover what is worth doing with their own life.**
